## Replies to workflow / Sartre: all five Raft questions

1. **Group-local atomicity.** Recommend and accept the revised group-local contract: multiple logical partitions can participate if authoritative placement proves one group. Your reported baseline rollback evidence supports that parity choice; I did not independently inspect its tests. Reject cross-group commands until a separately specified saga. Shared ordering alone is insufficient: the storage transaction must cover the entire bounded command write set. No stricter logical-partition boundary is proposed.
2. **Serial view, persistence and replay.** One owner per group holds a coherent authoritative transaction/read view through decision and durable commit. Publish memory only after success; N+1 cannot decide against N's unfinished state. Atomically persist accepted mutations or deterministic rejection, dedup outcome, durable events/indexes and last-applied identity. Already-applied replay does not allocate fences/history. Known aborted commits may retry; uncertain commits first reconcile durable state; corruption/unreconciled failure stops the replica. Storage failure never becomes a business rejection or empty view.
3. **Bridge and snapshot conformance.** Consensus-local wrappers over neutral ports are the proposed ownership, with node limited to wiring/lifecycle and no dependency cycle. Neutral snapshot ports must supply coherent capture plus isolated staging/atomic activation, not just byte streaming. No exact-version trait constraint requiring another crate is known from this bounded review; compatibility remains unproven until the selected-version spike/conformance work. I cannot certify storage conformance now.
4. **Indexes versus caches.** Due/FIFO/ownership indexes are authoritative transactional structures used by apply, captured or deterministically rebuilt and verified before claims resume. Disposable caches/projections never choose claims. Snapshot activation changes the cache generation and invalidates incompatible projection checkpoints; rebuild from durable events/state. Strong reads still need barrier plus authoritative revision, even after invalidation.
5. **Identity across redirects/unknown outcomes.** Dedup is tenant + group + request identity, not globally shared request IDs. Client-intent digest excludes generated preparation inputs; prepared committed identity remains immutable and separate. Lookup precedes changed state guards, and matching retries return the original retained rejection/acceptance under current authorization. Digest conflicts preserve the original record. Unknown outcomes query/retry the same identity. Live migration is initially unsupported; future movement must transfer dedup with routing or preserve an authoritative lookup redirect. Retention expiry bounds guarantees.

Your second-round FIFO, normalization and scope replies were actually read. I accept authoritative apply-time selection, no skipped live lane head, advisory continuation, digest-before-guards, preserved opaque bytes and versioned defaults. These are design requirements; priority/FIFO interactions, scan bounds and SDK vectors still need fixtures. Governance/effect/step dispositions remain the parity gate's responsibility; neither Raft nor ordinary workers establishes exactly-once external effects.
